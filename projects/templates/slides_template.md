# Presentation Slides Guide — Week 10

**Target: 5–7 slides · 10 minutes presentation + 5 minutes Q&A**

---

## Suggested Slide Structure

### Slide 1 — Title

- Project title
- Group member names
- Method used (e.g., "PINN for Heat Equation")

---

### Slide 2 — Problem and Motivation

- What problem are you solving?
- Why does it matter? (scientific or engineering context)
- What is your goal?

*Keep this concise: 3 bullets max*

---

### Slide 3 — Method

- Which method (MLP / CNN / VAE / Diffusion / PINN / RL)?
- Key design choices (architecture, loss, etc.)
- One diagram: data flow or model architecture

*Tip: Do not go deep into math. Focus on intuition.*

---

### Slide 4 — Dataset and Setup

- Data source and size
- Train/val/test split
- Key preprocessing steps
- Hardware and training time

---

### Slide 5 — Results

- Your primary quantitative result (table or number)
- Your most informative visualization
- Comparison if applicable

*One clear message per slide*

---

### Slide 6 — Discussion

- What worked?
- What was the main challenge?
- What would you do differently?

---

### Slide 7 — Conclusion and Connection

- 2-sentence summary of what you learned
- Connection to lab research or future direction
- Optional: next steps if the project were to continue

---

## Presentation Tips

- Practice your 10-minute timing before the session
- Every slide should have a clear message in the title
- Use code snippets sparingly — one key snippet maximum
- Prepare for these Q&A questions:
  - "Why did you choose this method over alternatives?"
  - "How would this scale to a real lab problem?"
  - "What would you need to improve the results?"
